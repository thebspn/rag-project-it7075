# Evolution Stages and Rule Box Mechanics

Pokemon cards are categorized into distinct evolutionary tiers: Basic, Stage 1, and Stage 2 Pokemon. Managing the timing and pace of evolutionary lines is central to high-level tournament deck strategy.

## Evolution Timing and Accelerants
A player cannot evolve a Pokemon on the very first turn that Pokemon is played to the field, nor can a player evolve a Pokemon on the opening turn of the match. To evolve legally, the higher stage card is placed directly on top of the matching prior stage. Evolving immediately cleanses all Special Conditions and removes lingering attack modifiers from the lower stage. However, all attached Energy cards, attached Pokemon Tools, and existing damage counters remain on the card.

Rare Candy is an indispensable staple Item card that accelerates evolution lines. Rare Candy allows a player to evolve a Basic Pokemon directly into a Stage 2 card from hand, bypassing the Stage 1 intermediate form entirely, provided the target Basic Pokemon was not placed into play during the current active turn.

## Pokemon ex and the Prize Trade Economy
Modern competitive environments feature Pokemon ex, distinguishable by extensive Hit Points (HP) and powerful multi-energy attacks balanced by an explicit Rule Box printed at the base of the card. Pokemon ex adhere to regular evolution staging—meaning a Stage 2 ex must evolve from a Stage 1 or via Rare Candy—unless specifically designated as a Basic Pokemon ex.

The central trade-off of Rule Box Pokemon is the Prize card penalty. When a standard single-prize Pokemon is Knocked Out, the opponent takes one Prize card. However, when a Pokemon ex is Knocked Out, the opponent takes two Prize cards. A match concludes the moment a competitor draws all six of their initial Prize cards.

# Deck Construction and Card Constraints

A legal Pokemon Trading Card Game deck for competitive play must contain exactly sixty cards. Players are strictly prohibited from running more or less than this precise number in sanctioned tournaments. 

## The Four-Copy Restriction
With the exception of Basic Energy cards, a player cannot include more than four copies of any card sharing the exact same name in their deck. This constraint applies universally regardless of whether the cards have different promotional artwork, alternate rarities, distinct expansions, or separate attack profiles. For example, a deck list cannot contain three copies of Charizard ex from Obsidian Flames and two copies of Charizard ex from 151, as the aggregate count exceeds the four-copy limit.

## Basic Energy and ACE SPEC Rules
Basic Energy cards (Grass, Fire, Water, Lightning, Psychic, Fighting, Darkness, and Metal) possess no copy limitations. A deck designer may include as many Basic Energy cards as necessary to fulfill deck sequencing goals. Special Energy cards, conversely, remain strictly bound by the standard four-copy maximum limit.

Modern Standard format sets introduced ACE SPEC cards, which represent exceptionally powerful Trainer or Special Energy cards with extreme deck construction restrictions. A player is permitted to include only one single ACE SPEC card in their entire sixty-card deck. If an ACE SPEC Trainer card such as Prime Catcher is included, no other ACE SPEC card of any type may be integrated into the deck list.

## Opening Hand Setup and Mulligans
Every legal deck must contain at least one Basic Pokemon card to ensure match setup is possible. At the start of a match, both competitors draw an opening hand of seven cards. If an opening hand contains zero Basic Pokemon, the player must declare a mulligan. The player reveals their hand to the opponent, shuffles the cards back into the deck, and draws seven new cards. For every mulligan declared, the opposing player may choose to draw one additional card during the opening setup phase after placing their initial active and bench cards face down.
